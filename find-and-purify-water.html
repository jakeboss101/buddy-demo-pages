How to Find and Purify Water in the Wild

October 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Survival experts talk about the rule of threes: you can last about three hours without shelter in harsh weather, three days without water, and three weeks without food. That makes water your first job if you're stuck outdoors. Even clear mountain streams can carry germs like Giardia, so plan to treat every drop.

The short version
  • Look downhill, follow green plants and listen for running water.
  • Pick moving water over still water, and collect it upstream of animals and campsites.
  • Boil it for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft / 2,000 m). That's the surest method.

Where to find water

  • Go downhill. Water collects in valleys, gullies and the bottoms of slopes.
  • Follow the green. Thick, bright plants, willows and cattails grow near water.
  • Watch animals. Animal tracks and birds circling at dawn or dusk often lead to water.
  • Catch the rain. Spread a tarp, poncho or trash bag and funnel it into a bottle.
  • Collect dew. At sunrise, wipe dew off grass with a clean cloth and wring it out.

Four ways to make it safe

1. Boil it

Bring water to a rolling boil for 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 6,500 feet. Boiling kills bacteria, viruses and parasites. Let it cool before drinking.

2. Filter it

A hiking filter with a pore size of 0.1 to 1 micron removes bacteria and parasites, but most don't stop viruses. Pair it with tablets if you're not sure of the source.

3. Use tablets

Chlorine dioxide tablets work against most germs. Follow the package. Most need 30 minutes, and up to 4 hours to kill tough parasites like Cryptosporidium.

4. Household bleach (emergency only)

Use plain, unscented bleach (6–8%): about 2 drops per liter, or 8 drops per gallon. Stir, then wait 30 minutes. It should smell slightly of chlorine. If not, repeat and wait another 15 minutes.

Cloudy water?

Let it settle, then pour it through a clean cloth or coffee filter first. Mud and grit make every method work less well.

Never drink

  • Seawater: the salt dehydrates you faster.
  • Water near mines, farms or factories: boiling doesn't remove chemicals.
  • Snow without melting it: eating snow lowers your body temperature.

Pack a small filter and a few tablets on every trip. They weigh almost nothing and can save your trip, or more.
