Lost on the Trail? What to Do, Step by Step

October 15, 2026 · 6 min read

It happens to experienced hikers too: a missed turn, fog rolling in, a trail that slowly disappears. Getting lost isn't the dangerous part. Panicking is. Most lost hikers are found within a day, especially when they stay put.

Remember STOP
  • Stop: sit down, drink some water, eat a snack. Breathe.
  • Think: when did you last know where you were?
  • Observe: look for landmarks, the sun's direction, trail markers, sounds of roads or water.
  • Plan: decide on one calm next step. Usually that's staying where you are.

Call or text for help

Try 911 even with one bar. In many areas you can text 911, and texts often get through when calls don't. Share your location and stay on the line if you can. Then save battery: turn on battery saver mode, lower the screen brightness, and check the phone every hour rather than every minute.

Stay put (usually)

Rescuers start searching from your last known point. Walking around makes you harder to find and wears you out. Only move if you're in danger, or you can clearly see the trail or a road.

Make yourself easy to find

  • Three of anything means "help": three whistle blasts, three shouts, three fires or rock piles.
  • Blow a whistle instead of yelling. It carries farther and saves your voice.
  • Spread bright clothing or a tarp in an open area that's visible from above.
  • Use a mirror or your phone screen to flash sunlight toward aircraft.

Stay warm and dry

  • Get out of the wind behind rocks, a fallen log or thick trees.
  • Insulate yourself from the ground with branches, leaves or your pack. The cold ground drains heat fast.
  • Put on every layer before you get cold, and keep your head covered.
  • Huddle together if you're with others.

Before your next hike

  • Tell someone your route and when you'll be back.
  • Download an offline map and carry a paper map and compass.
  • Pack a whistle, headlamp, extra layer, water and a space blanket.

Stay calm, stay put, and make noise in threes. Help is on its way.
